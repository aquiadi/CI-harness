Synthetic guidance for installation operators outside the customs territory

1 Why you are being asked for data
An importer in the customs territory has to report the emissions embedded in the
goods it buys from you. It cannot do that without data from the installation
where the goods were made. You are not directly subject to the synthetic
Regulation, and no penalty under it applies to you, but the importer's ability
to keep buying from you depends on the data you provide.

2 What an installation is
A stationary technical unit where a production process is carried out. If your
site runs several production processes, each is reported separately, with its
own activity level and its own attributed emissions.

3 Identifying your installation
Give the importer the installation name and address, the geographical
coordinates of the main emission source, and the United Nations code for trade
and transport locations for the site. These identify the installation across
reports from different importers, so use the same values each time.

4 Determining direct emissions
Direct emissions are the emissions of the production process, including
emissions from heating and cooling consumed in the process, wherever that
heating and cooling was produced. Determine the attributed emissions of the
process and divide by the activity level of the process for the reporting
period. Report the result per tonne of goods.

5 Determining indirect emissions
Indirect emissions come from the electricity consumed in the production process.
Use the actual emission factor of the electricity you consumed where you can
demonstrate a direct technical link or a power purchase agreement; otherwise use
the average emission factor of the grid in your country. Say which of the two
you used and why, because the importer has to report that basis.

6 Precursors
If your product is a complex good, the emissions embedded in the precursors you
consumed count towards its embedded emissions. For each precursor, record its
type, quantity, country of origin, the installation that produced it, and its
embedded emissions. If you buy a precursor from another installation, you will
need the same data from them that the importer needs from you.

7 Monitoring periods
Use a monitoring period of one calendar year, or a period of at least three
months ending no more than one year before the reporting quarter, where annual
data is not available. State the period you used. Do not mix periods across
production processes without saying so.

8 Verification from 2026
From 2026 the importer's annual declaration must be verified by an accredited
verifier, and the verifier will need access to your underlying data. A verifier
with a financial interest in your installation cannot verify your emissions.
Preparing for that now is cheaper than preparing for it in 2027.

9 Carbon prices you already pay
If your emissions are subject to a carbon price where you are, tell the importer
the price due for the emissions concerned and whether any rebate or other
compensation is available on export. The importer can only claim a reduction
where the price was effectively paid and no export rebate was available.

10 What happens if you provide nothing
The importer records the request and the absence of a response, and falls back
to default values. Default values are set at the average emission intensity of
your country for that type of goods, increased by a mark-up. If reliable country
data is not available, they are based on the average of the ten worst performing
installations in the customs territory. Both are usually worse for you than your
actual figures.

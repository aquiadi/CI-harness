Synthetic Implementing Regulation on reporting obligations during the
transitional period

Article 1
Subject matter
This synthetic Implementing Regulation lays down rules for the reporting
obligations of reporting declarants during the transitional period, covering
the content and format of the CBAM report, the procedure for its submission,
the methods for calculating embedded emissions, and the penalties applicable
where a report is not submitted or is incorrect.

Article 2
Content of the CBAM report
The CBAM report shall contain, for each type of goods, the quantity imported
expressed in megawatt hours for electricity and in tonnes for other goods; the
type of goods identified by the customs nomenclature code; the country of
origin; the installation where the goods were produced, identified by the
United Nations code for trade and transport locations and by the geographical
coordinates of the main emission source; the production routes used; the total
embedded direct emissions and the total embedded indirect emissions, expressed
in tonnes of carbon dioxide equivalent per tonne of goods; and the carbon price
due in the country of origin, together with a description of any rebate or other
form of compensation available.

Article 3
Submission of the CBAM report
The reporting declarant shall submit the CBAM report through the CBAM
transitional registry no later than one month after the end of the quarter to
which it relates. A report shall be considered submitted when it has been
assigned a unique report identification number by the registry.

Article 4
Modification and correction of reports
The reporting declarant may modify a submitted CBAM report until two months
after the end of the relevant reporting quarter. By way of derogation, the
reporting declarant may modify the CBAM reports for the first two reporting
periods until the submission deadline of the third CBAM report. Beyond those
deadlines, the reporting declarant may request the competent authority to
correct a report within one year of the end of the relevant quarter, where the
declarant demonstrates that the report could not be modified through the
registry for technical reasons or that the correction is required to reflect
information that became available only later.

Article 5
Methods for determining embedded emissions
Until 31 December 2024, a reporting declarant may determine embedded emissions
using one of the following methods: the method set out in Annex III of the
synthetic Regulation; a carbon pricing scheme where the installation is located,
or a compulsory emission monitoring scheme where the installation is located, or
an emission monitoring scheme at the installation which can include verification
by an accredited verifier; or, for reports due for periods ending before 31 July
2024 only, estimated values published by the Commission for the transitional
period.

Article 6
Use of estimated values
From 1 January 2025, only the method set out in Annex III of the synthetic
Regulation may be used. By way of derogation, until 31 December 2025 estimated
values may be used for up to 20 per cent of the total embedded emissions of
complex goods, where the reporting declarant records the reason why actual data
could not be obtained from the operator.

Article 7
Data to be obtained from operators
The reporting declarant shall request from the operator of the installation the
information listed in Annex I to this synthetic Implementing Regulation. Where
the operator does not provide that information, the reporting declarant shall
record the request and the absence of a response, and shall state in the CBAM
report which method was used instead.

Article 8
Incomplete reports
A CBAM report is incomplete where more than one of the mandatory data fields in
Article 2 is missing for a given type of goods, or where the report does not
allow the quantity of imported goods to be determined. A CBAM report is
incorrect where a mandatory data field contains information that does not
correspond to the goods imported.

Article 9
Correction procedure initiated by the competent authority
Where the competent authority finds that a CBAM report is incomplete or
incorrect, it shall communicate the assessment to the reporting declarant and
request the declarant to submit a corrected report. The declarant shall be given
a period of no less than 15 and no more than 30 calendar days to submit the
corrected report.

Article 10
Penalties
A penalty of between EUR 10 and EUR 50 per tonne of unreported embedded
emissions shall be imposed where the reporting declarant has not taken the
necessary steps to comply with the obligation to submit a CBAM report, or where
the report is incorrect or incomplete and the declarant has not taken the
necessary steps to correct it following the correction procedure. The penalty
shall be higher where more than two incomplete or incorrect reports have been
submitted in a row, or where the failure to report lasts for more than six
months. Amounts are indexed to the European index of consumer prices.

Article 11
Factors to be taken into account when setting a penalty
In determining the amount of the penalty, the competent authority shall take
into account the extent of the unreported information, the quantities of goods
concerned and the emissions relating to those goods, the willingness of the
reporting declarant to comply with requests for information, the intentional or
negligent conduct of the declarant, the past behaviour of the declarant, the
level of cooperation of the declarant to bring the infringement to an end, and
whether the declarant has voluntarily taken measures to prevent similar
infringements in the future.

ANNEX I
Information to be requested from operators

Identification of the installation, including its name, address, geographical
coordinates and the United Nations code for trade and transport locations.
Production routes used and, for each production route, the activity level and
the attributed direct emissions of the production process. The quantity of
electricity consumed in the production process and the emission factor applied,
together with the basis on which that factor was chosen. For each precursor
consumed, its type, quantity, country of origin, installation of production and
embedded emissions. Where applicable, the carbon price due in the country of
origin for the emissions concerned, and any rebate or other form of compensation
available.

ANNEX II
Reporting periods and deadlines

The first reporting period runs from 1 October 2023 to 31 December 2023, with a
report due by 31 January 2024. The second runs from 1 January 2024 to 31 March
2024, with a report due by 30 April 2024. The third runs from 1 April 2024 to 30
June 2024, with a report due by 31 July 2024. Each subsequent quarter follows
the same pattern until the reporting period ending 31 December 2025, for which
the report is due by 31 January 2026.

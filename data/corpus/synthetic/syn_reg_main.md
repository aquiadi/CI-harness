Synthetic Regulation on a carbon border adjustment mechanism

CHAPTER I
Subject matter, scope and definitions

Article 1
Subject matter
This synthetic Regulation establishes a carbon border adjustment mechanism for
addressing embedded emissions in the goods listed in Annex I on their
importation into the customs territory, with a view to preventing the risk of
carbon leakage and thereby reducing global carbon emissions. The mechanism
applies in parallel with the emissions trading system and is designed so that
imported goods bear a carbon price equivalent to the price borne by goods
produced within the customs territory.

Article 2
Scope
This Regulation applies to goods listed in Annex I originating in a third
country, where those goods, or processed products from those goods resulting
from the inward processing procedure, are imported into the customs territory.
It does not apply to goods whose total intrinsic value, per consignment, does
not exceed EUR 150. It does not apply to goods contained in the personal
baggage of travellers coming from a third country, where the intrinsic value of
those goods does not exceed the same threshold. It does not apply to goods to
be transported or used in the context of military activities.

Article 3
Definitions
For the purposes of this Regulation the following definitions apply.
"Goods" means the goods listed in Annex I.
"Embedded emissions" means direct emissions released during the production of
goods and, for the goods so identified in Annex I, the indirect emissions from
the production of electricity consumed during the production processes.
"Direct emissions" means emissions from the production processes of goods,
including emissions from the production of heating and cooling consumed during
those processes, irrespective of the location of the production of that heating
and cooling.
"Indirect emissions" means emissions from the production of electricity
consumed during the production processes of goods, irrespective of the location
of production of that electricity.
"Authorised CBAM declarant" means a person authorised by a competent authority
in accordance with Article 9.
"Installation" means a stationary technical unit where a production process is
carried out.
"Operator" means any person who operates or controls an installation in a third
country.
"Reporting declarant" means, during the transitional period, the importer
lodging a customs declaration for release for free circulation in its own name
and on its own behalf, or the indirect customs representative where the importer
is established outside the customs territory.

CHAPTER II
Obligations and rights of authorised declarants

Article 4
Importation of goods
Goods shall be imported into the customs territory only by an authorised CBAM
declarant. This Article applies from 1 January 2026 and does not apply during
the transitional period.

Article 5
Application for authorisation
Any importer established in a Member State shall, prior to importing goods,
apply for the status of authorised CBAM declarant. Where the importer is not
established in a Member State, the indirect customs representative shall apply.
An application shall be submitted through the CBAM registry and shall contain
the applicant's name, address and contact details, the applicant's economic
operator registration and identification number, the applicant's principal
economic activity, an estimate of the quantity and type of goods to be imported
in the calendar year during which the application is submitted and in the
following calendar year, and the names and contact details of the persons on
whose behalf the applicant is acting where applicable.

Article 6
CBAM declaration
By 31 May of each year, and for the first time in 2027 for the year 2026, each
authorised CBAM declarant shall use the CBAM registry to submit a CBAM
declaration for the preceding calendar year. The CBAM declaration shall
contain the total quantity of each type of goods imported during the preceding
calendar year, expressed in megawatt hours for electricity and in tonnes for
other goods; the total embedded emissions in those goods, expressed in tonnes of
carbon dioxide equivalent emissions per megawatt hour of electricity or, for
other goods, in tonnes of carbon dioxide equivalent emissions per tonne of each
type of goods; the total number of CBAM certificates to be surrendered,
corresponding to the total embedded emissions after the reduction due on account
of the carbon price paid in a country of origin and after the adjustment
necessary to reflect the extent to which allowances are allocated free of charge
under the emissions trading system; and copies of verification reports issued by
accredited verifiers.

Article 7
Calculation of embedded emissions
Embedded emissions in goods shall be calculated in accordance with the methods
set out in Annex III. Embedded emissions in simple goods shall be calculated on
the basis of the actual emissions of the installation where the goods were
produced. Embedded emissions in complex goods shall take into account the
embedded emissions of the relevant precursor materials consumed in the
production process. Where actual emissions cannot be adequately determined by
the authorised CBAM declarant, default values shall be used. Those default
values shall be set at the average emission intensity of each exporting country
and for each of the goods listed in Annex I, increased by a mark-up determined
in implementing acts. Where reliable data for an exporting country cannot be
applied for a type of goods, the default values shall be based on the average
emission intensity of the ten worst performing installations in the customs
territory for that type of goods.

Article 8
Verification of embedded emissions
The authorised CBAM declarant shall ensure that the total embedded emissions
declared in the CBAM declaration are verified by a verifier accredited in
accordance with Article 18. The verifier shall issue a verification report
containing an opinion stating with reasonable assurance whether the declared
embedded emissions are free from material misstatement. A verifier shall not
verify emissions of an installation in which it has a financial interest.

Article 9
Authorisation
The competent authority shall grant the status of authorised CBAM declarant to
an applicant who satisfies the criteria in this Article. The competent authority
shall consult, through the CBAM registry, the competent authorities in the other
Member States and shall take a decision within 120 calendar days of receipt of
a complete application. Where the competent authority requires additional
information in order to process an application, that period shall be suspended
from the date on which the request is sent until the date on which the
information is received. The status shall be granted where the applicant has not
been involved in a serious infringement or repeated infringements of customs
legislation, taxation rules or market abuse rules during the five years
preceding the year of the application; demonstrates financial and operational
capacity to fulfil its obligations under this Regulation; and is established in
the Member State where the application is submitted. Where the applicant has not
been established for the whole of the five years preceding the application, the
competent authority shall assess the criteria on the basis of the records and
information that are available to it.

Article 10
Revocation of authorisation
The competent authority shall revoke the status of authorised CBAM declarant
where the holder no longer satisfies the criteria set out in Article 9, or
where the holder has failed to comply with the obligation to surrender CBAM
certificates, or has failed to submit a CBAM declaration by the deadline in
Article 6 for two consecutive years. Before revoking, the competent authority
shall give the holder the opportunity to be heard and shall state the reasons
for its decision. A decision to revoke may be appealed.

CHAPTER III
CBAM certificates

Article 11
Sale of certificates
Member States shall sell CBAM certificates to authorised CBAM declarants
through a common central platform. A certificate corresponds to one tonne of
carbon dioxide equivalent embedded emissions in goods.

Article 12
Price of certificates
The price of CBAM certificates shall be calculated as the average of the closing
prices of emission allowances on the common auction platform for each calendar
week. For a calendar week in which no auctions are scheduled, the price shall be
the average of the closing prices of the last calendar week in which auctions
were scheduled.

Article 13
Surrender of certificates
By 31 May of each year, and for the first time in 2027, each authorised CBAM
declarant shall surrender, through the CBAM registry, a number of CBAM
certificates corresponding to the embedded emissions declared in the CBAM
declaration for the preceding calendar year. At the end of each quarter, an
authorised CBAM declarant shall ensure that the number of CBAM certificates on
its account in the registry corresponds to at least 80 per cent of the embedded
emissions in all goods it has imported since the beginning of the calendar year.

Article 14
Repurchase of certificates
On request by an authorised CBAM declarant, a Member State shall repurchase the
excess CBAM certificates remaining on the declarant's account after the
certificates have been surrendered. The request shall be submitted by 30 June of
each year in which certificates were surrendered. The number of certificates
subject to repurchase shall be limited to one third of the total number of CBAM
certificates purchased by the authorised CBAM declarant during the previous
calendar year. Repurchase shall be at the price paid for those certificates at
the time of purchase.

Article 15
Cancellation of certificates
By 1 July of each year, the Commission shall cancel any CBAM certificates that
were purchased during the year before the previous calendar year and that remain
on the account of an authorised CBAM declarant. Those certificates shall be
cancelled without any compensation.

Article 16
Carbon price paid in a country of origin
An authorised CBAM declarant may claim in its CBAM declaration a reduction in
the number of CBAM certificates to be surrendered, corresponding to the carbon
price effectively paid in the country of origin for the declared embedded
emissions. The reduction may be claimed only where the carbon price has been
effectively paid and where no export rebate or other form of compensation on
exportation was available in that country. The authorised CBAM declarant shall
keep records of the documentation required to demonstrate that the declared
embedded emissions were subject to a carbon price, certified by an independent
person.

CHAPTER IV
Transitional period

Article 17
Transitional period
The transitional period runs from 1 October 2023 to 31 December 2025. During the
transitional period the obligations of an importer are limited to the reporting
obligations set out in Article 18. No CBAM certificates shall be sold or
surrendered during the transitional period, and no financial adjustment is due.

Article 18
Reporting obligation during the transitional period
Each reporting declarant shall, for each quarter of a calendar year, submit a
CBAM report containing information on the goods imported during that quarter, no
later than one month after the end of that quarter. The CBAM report shall
contain the total quantity of each type of goods, the total embedded direct
emissions, the total embedded indirect emissions, and the carbon price due in a
country of origin for the embedded emissions, taking into account any rebate or
other form of compensation available.

ANNEX I
Goods and greenhouse gases

The following goods are covered: cement; electricity; fertilisers; iron and
steel; aluminium; hydrogen. Indirect emissions are covered for cement,
fertilisers and electricity. For iron and steel, aluminium and hydrogen, only
direct emissions are covered during the transitional period. The greenhouse
gases covered are carbon dioxide for all goods, nitrous oxide for fertilisers,
and perfluorocarbons for aluminium.

ANNEX II
Third countries and territories outside the scope

Goods originating in Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway and Switzerland are outside
the scope of this synthetic Regulation, on the basis that those countries or
territories participate in the emissions trading system or have an emissions
trading system linked to it.

ANNEX III
Methods for calculating embedded emissions

Direct emissions of simple goods shall be determined at installation level as
the attributed emissions of the production process divided by the activity level
of that process. Indirect emissions shall be determined from the electricity
consumed in the production process multiplied by an emission factor, which shall
be either the actual emission factor of the electricity consumed, where a direct
technical link or a power purchase agreement can be demonstrated, or the average
emission factor of the electricity grid in the country of origin. For complex
goods, the embedded emissions of the precursors consumed shall be added to the
attributed emissions of the production process itself.

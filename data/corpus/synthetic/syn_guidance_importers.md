Synthetic guidance for importers of goods covered by the mechanism

1 Purpose of this guidance
This guidance explains in plain language what an importer has to do under the
synthetic Regulation and the synthetic Implementing Regulation. Where this
guidance and those documents differ, the documents govern. It is written for
people who import goods and who are not lawyers.

2 Do the rules apply to me
They apply if you import cement, electricity, fertilisers, iron and steel,
aluminium or hydrogen into the customs territory. They do not apply if the total
value of a consignment is EUR 150 or less. They do not apply to goods you carry
in personal baggage below that value, or to goods for military use. If you buy
covered goods from a supplier inside the customs territory, you are not the
importer and the reporting obligation is not yours.

3 What changes and when
From 1 October 2023 to 31 December 2025 you only report. You do not buy
certificates and you do not pay anything under the mechanism. From 1 January
2026 you must be an authorised declarant before you import, you submit an annual
declaration, and you surrender certificates for the emissions you declared.

4 Quarterly reporting during the transitional period
You submit one report per quarter through the transitional registry, within one
month of the quarter ending. So the report for January to March is due by 30
April. You report how much of each good you imported, where it was made, which
production route was used, and the emissions embedded in it, split into direct
and indirect. You also report any carbon price you already paid in the country
where the goods were made.

5 If you get a report wrong
You can change a submitted report yourself for two months after the quarter
ends. For the first two reporting periods only, you have longer: you can change
those reports up to the deadline for the third report. After that you have to
ask the competent authority, which can allow a correction within one year of the
end of the quarter if you can show the registry would not let you make the
change or that the information only became available later.

6 Getting emissions data from your supplier
Ask the operator of the installation for the data listed in Annex I of the
synthetic Implementing Regulation. If they will not give it to you, keep a
record of the request and of the fact you got no answer, and say in your report
which method you used instead. Until the end of 2024 you have several methods
available, including a monitoring scheme run at the installation. From 1 January
2025 you must use the method in Annex III of the synthetic Regulation, with a
narrow allowance for estimated values covering at most 20 per cent of the
embedded emissions of complex goods, and only until the end of 2025.

7 Becoming an authorised declarant
Apply through the registry before you import, from 2026. The authority has 120
days to decide, and that clock stops while it is waiting for information from
you. You must be established in the Member State where you apply, you must show
you can meet your obligations financially and operationally, and you must not
have a record of serious or repeated infringements of customs, tax or market
abuse rules in the five years before you apply. If you are not established in
the customs territory, your indirect customs representative applies instead of
you.

8 Certificates from 2026
You buy certificates from your Member State through a common central platform.
One certificate covers one tonne of carbon dioxide equivalent. The price follows
the weekly average closing price of emission allowances. At the end of each
quarter, you must hold certificates covering at least 80 per cent of the
emissions embedded in what you have imported so far that year. By 31 May you
declare the previous year and surrender the certificates that match it.

9 Getting money back
If you hold more certificates than you needed, you can ask your Member State to
buy back the excess. Ask by 30 June of the year in which you surrendered. The
buy-back is capped at one third of what you bought during the previous calendar
year, and you get back what you paid. Anything left over from two years earlier
is cancelled on 1 July with no compensation, so do not over-buy.

10 If you already paid a carbon price abroad
You can reduce the certificates you surrender by the carbon price you actually
paid in the country of origin for the same emissions. You have to have actually
paid it, and there must not have been a rebate or other compensation on export.
Keep documentation, certified by an independent person.

11 Penalties during the transitional period
If you do not report, or you report something incomplete or incorrect and do not
fix it when asked, the penalty is between EUR 10 and EUR 50 per tonne of
unreported emissions. It goes up if you have submitted more than two bad reports
in a row or if you have not reported for more than six months. The authority
takes into account how much was missing, whether you cooperated, and whether it
was deliberate.

12 Common mistakes
Reporting electricity in tonnes rather than megawatt hours. Assuming the
estimated values published for the transitional period remain available after 31
July 2024. Assuming indirect emissions are reported for iron and steel during
the transitional period, when only direct emissions are. Forgetting that the
EUR 150 threshold is per consignment, not per year.

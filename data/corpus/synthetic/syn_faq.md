Synthetic questions and answers on the mechanism

Q1 Is the mechanism a tax
No. It is not levied at the border as a duty. During the transitional period
nothing is payable at all. From 2026 the importer buys certificates from its
Member State and surrenders them once a year against the emissions it declared,
which is closer to how allowances work under the emissions trading system than
to how a customs duty works.

Q2 We import small quantities. Are we caught
Only if a consignment exceeds EUR 150 in total intrinsic value. The threshold is
per consignment. Splitting a shipment to stay under it is not a strategy anyone
should rely on, and repeated consignments to the same buyer may be looked at
together.

Q3 We are established outside the customs territory. Who reports
Your indirect customs representative. During the transitional period, if the
importer is not established in the customs territory, the reporting declarant is
the indirect customs representative where that representative agrees to act. From
2026 the indirect customs representative is also the one who applies for
authorised declarant status.

Q4 Which emissions do we have to report for steel
During the transitional period, direct emissions only. Indirect emissions are
reported for cement, fertilisers and electricity. Iron and steel, aluminium and
hydrogen are direct only during that period. This is one of the most common
sources of over-reporting.

Q5 Our supplier will not give us emissions data
Ask in writing, keep the request and the absence of a reply, and use a fallback
method. Until 31 December 2024 several fallback methods are available. From 1
January 2025 you must use the standard method, with a limited allowance for
estimated values covering at most 20 per cent of the embedded emissions of
complex goods, and only to the end of 2025. Falling back usually produces a
higher figure than actual data, because default values carry a mark-up.

Q6 What is a complex good
A good whose production consumes precursor materials that are themselves covered
goods. Its embedded emissions include the embedded emissions of those
precursors, not only the emissions of the final production process. A simple
good has no such precursors, and its embedded emissions are the attributed
emissions of its own process divided by the activity level.

Q7 We paid a carbon price in the country of production. Does that help
From 2026, yes. You can reduce the number of certificates you surrender by the
carbon price effectively paid for the same emissions, provided no export rebate
or other compensation was available in that country. You need documentation
certified by an independent person. During the transitional period you report
the carbon price due but there is nothing to reduce, because nothing is
surrendered.

Q8 How is the certificate price set
As the average of the closing prices of emission allowances on the common
auction platform for each calendar week. In a week with no auctions, the price
is the average of the last week that had them. There is no separate market and
no secondary trading of certificates between declarants.

Q9 What if we buy too many certificates
Ask your Member State to buy back the excess by 30 June of the year in which you
surrendered. The buy-back is capped at one third of what you bought in the
previous calendar year and is at the price you paid. Certificates from two years
earlier are cancelled on 1 July with no compensation.

Q10 What is the 80 per cent rule
At the end of each quarter from 2026, the certificates on your registry account
must cover at least 80 per cent of the emissions embedded in everything you have
imported since the start of that calendar year. It is a running holding
requirement through the year, separate from the annual surrender by 31 May.

Q11 We missed a quarterly report during the transitional period
Submit it. The penalty is between EUR 10 and EUR 50 per tonne of unreported
embedded emissions, and it is higher if you have missed more than six months or
submitted more than two bad reports in a row. The authority weighs how much was
missing, whether you cooperated, and whether the failure was deliberate or
negligent.

Q12 Can our authorisation be taken away
Yes. The status is revoked if you stop meeting the criteria, if you fail to
surrender certificates, or if you miss the annual declaration deadline two years
running. You get the chance to be heard first and the decision must give
reasons, and it can be appealed.

Q13 Do goods from Norway or Switzerland count
No. Goods originating in Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway and Switzerland are
outside scope, because those countries participate in the emissions trading
system or have a system linked to it.

Q14 What exactly is an installation
A stationary technical unit where a production process is carried out. It is
identified by name, address, geographical coordinates of the main emission
source, and the United Nations trade and transport location code. Use the same
identifiers in every report, because that is how emissions from one site are
reconciled across different importers.
